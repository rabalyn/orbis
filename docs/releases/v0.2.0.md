# Orbis 0.2.0

A release about speed: the desktop app loads about twice as fast and no longer freezes at the start, and the energy bars keep their frame rate at every level of detail.

## Highlights

- In the desktop app, tiles appear about twice as fast. Their road and boundary overlays were being drawn one at a time; they are now drawn in parallel, on as many threads as the machine offers.
- The desktop window no longer freezes for a quarter of a second at the start: the energy data is read in the background.
- The energy bars stay smooth at the Plants and Municipalities levels, also zoomed far out with every plant site in view. Before, the Plants level could fall to a few frames a second.
- The flat map costs a fraction of the processor time while the view is at rest, and so do the panels.
- In the desktop app on a real graphics card, roads and boundaries are painted onto the tiles by the card. `--cpu-overlays` goes back to the processor; `--gpu-overlays` forces the card.
- New for measuring: `--benchmark` reports the work time per frame, its parts and the slowest frames; `--fullscreen` and `--energy-detail LEVEL` set up a run; the `profile` build feature writes a flamegraph (see `docs/TESTING.md`).

## Fixes

- Pictures made with `--screenshot` are the same from one run to the next again: the day chart's window could come out a little wider or not, and bars could be drawn before the terrain under them had arrived.

## Notes

- Lines painted by the graphics card look like the processor's but are not identical pixel for pixel. `--screenshot` and the browser build keep to the processor.
- On a desktop with many processor cores, painting lines on the graphics card saves processor time but does not make tiles appear sooner.
- Known automated testing gaps are listed in `docs/TESTING.md`.
